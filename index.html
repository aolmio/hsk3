💡 Need-to-Know Facts & Essential Mandarin Mechanics

Crucial linguistic laws, HSK 2.0 vs 3.0 vs 3.1 differences, tone sandhi rules, and test strategies.

Pronunciation Law

1. Tone Sandhi Rules (变调 Biàndiào)

  • 3rd + 3rd Tone Rule: When two 3rd tones meet, the first becomes a 2nd tone: 你好 (nǐ + hǎo → ní hǎo).
  • “不” (bù) Tone Change: Changes to 2nd tone (bú) before another 4th tone: 不是 (bù + shì → bú shì).
  • “一” (yī) Tone Change: 1st tone when counting/end of word; 2nd tone before a 4th tone (一个 → yí gè); 4th tone before 1st/2nd/3rd tones (一天 → yì tiān, 一起 → yì qǐ).

Syllabus Evolution

2. HSK 2.0 vs HSK 3.0 (2021) vs HSK 3.1 (2025)

  • HSK 2.0: Classic 6-level structure totaling 5,000 words.
  • HSK 3.0 (2021 Standard): Expanded to 3 stages & 9 levels (Levels 7–9 combined into one advanced exam) with 11,092 words.
  • HSK 3.1 (2025 Revision): Refines optional word segments (e.g., 没（有）, 有（一）点儿) and adjusts level placements for practical test alignment.
Grammar Trap

3. The Three “De” Particles (的 / 得 / 地)

  • 白勺 的 (de): Possessive / noun modifier (我的书 - My book).
  • 双人 得 (de): Degree complement after verbs (说得很好 - Speaks very well).
  • 土也 地 (de): Adverbial marker before verbs (认真地学习 - Study seriously).
Character Science

4. Hanzi Radicals & Stroke Order

  • 80% Phono-Semantic Compounds: Most characters pair a meaning radical (e.g., 氵water, 扌hand) with a phonetic sound component.
  • Standard Stroke Order Rules: Top to bottom, left to right, horizontal before vertical, outside before inside, close frame last.
