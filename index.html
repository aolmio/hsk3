💡 Need-to-Know Facts & Essential Mandarin Mechanics

Crucial linguistic rules, exam traps, tone sandhi laws, and syllabus differences between HSK 2.0, HSK 3.0 (2021), and HSK 3.1 (2025).

Pronunciation Law

1. Tone Sandhi Rules (变调 Biàndiào)

  • 3rd + 3rd Tone Rule: When two 3rd tones meet, the first becomes a 2nd tone: 你好 (nǐ + hǎo → ní hǎo).
  • “不” (bù) Tone Change: Changes to 2nd tone (bú) before another 4th tone: 不是 (bù + shì → bú shì).
  • “一” (yī) Tone Change: 1st tone when counting, 2nd tone before a 4th tone (一个 → yí gè), and 4th tone before 1st/2nd/3rd tones (一天 → yì tiān).

Syllabus Evolution

2. HSK 2.0 vs HSK 3.0 vs HSK 3.1

  • HSK 2.0 (Classic 1–6): Contains 5,000 total words across 6 levels. Still widely used in standard textbooks.
  • HSK 3.0 (2021 Standard): Expands to 9 bands (11,092 words) with explicit Character Recognition (认读字) and Handwritten Character (手写字) lists.
  • HSK 3.1 (2025/2026 Revision): Streamlines entry-level word counts (e.g., 300 words in Level 1 instead of 500) to make beginner progression smoother while keeping the 9-band architecture.
Test Strategy

3. Paper-Based vs Computer-Based (机考)

  • Pinyin Input Advantage: On the computer-based exam, you type characters using Pinyin input and select from candidates rather than writing strokes from memory.
  • Handwriting Requirement in 3.0: HSK 3.0 introduces a dedicated list of must-write characters (手写字) per level, accessible directly in the Character List tab.
Grammar Trap

4. The Three “De” Particles (的 / 得 / 地)

  • 白勺 的 (de): Noun modifier (我的书 - My book).
  • 双人 得 (de): Verb complement describing degree/result (跑得很快 - Runs fast).
  • 土也 地 (de): Adverbial marker before verbs (认真地学习 - Study seriously).
